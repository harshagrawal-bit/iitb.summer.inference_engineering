Six-Day Programme Schedule

21–26 June 2026. Mornings are lecture-led by the international faculty; afternoons are hands-on labs on the C-MInDS GPU cluster. All times are IST.

Daily rhythm: Registration & high-tea from 08:30 · Lectures 09:00–13:00 · Lunch break 13:00–14:00 (own arrangement) · Supervised labs 14:00–17:30 · Optional evening clinic 18:00–19:00.

08:30 – 09:15

Registration & welcome high-tea

Collect your kit and lab credentials at the VMCC foyer.

09:15 – 10:00

Inauguration & the India edition Keynote

Prof. Sunita Sarawagi · Convener (IIT Bombay) · with the international faculty

Why inference engineering matters, why it has come to India, and how the six days are structured.

10:00 – 11:00

Foundations of model inference

Prof. Sunita Sarawagi · IIT Bombay

The forward pass in detail; latency vs. throughput; tail latency; the cost equation for serving.

11:00 – 11:20

Tea break

11:20 – 13:00

Performance modelling & the roofline

Prof. Rio Yokota · Institute of Science Tokyo

Compute- vs. memory-bound regimes, arithmetic intensity, and reading a roofline plot for a real model.

13:00 – 14:00

Lunch break own arrangement

Campus canteens, hostel mess and venue counters are open.

14:00 – 17:30

Lab 0 — environment & first profile Hands-on

Lab leads & teaching assistants

Set up your GPU instance and profile a ResNet and a small transformer with the PyTorch profiler and Nsight Systems.

18:00 – 19:00

Welcome reception & optional clinic

Meet the faculty and cohort; open office-hours for setup.

09:00 – 10:30

Quantization: PTQ & QAT Intl. faculty

Prof. Song Han · MIT, USA

Post-training vs. quantization-aware training; INT8 and INT4; calibration; the accuracy–latency trade-off.

10:30 – 11:00

Pruning & sparsity

Prof. Song Han · MIT, USA

Structured vs. unstructured pruning and hardware-aware sparsity.

11:00 – 11:20

Tea break

11:20 – 13:00

Knowledge distillation & low-rank methods

Prof. Ganesh Ramakrishnan · IIT Bombay

Distilling large models into deployable students; LoRA-style adapters at inference time.

13:00 – 14:00

Lunch break own arrangement

14:00 – 17:30

Lab 1 — quantize & shrink Hands-on

Lab leads & teaching assistants

Quantize a transformer to INT8/INT4 and measure the speed-up and accuracy delta on your A100 instance.

18:00 – 19:00

Optional evening clinic

09:00 – 10:30

The CUDA execution model Intl. faculty

Prof. Rio Yokota · Institute of Science Tokyo

Threads, warps, memory hierarchy and coalescing — what actually happens on the GPU.

10:30 – 11:00

Operator fusion & FlashAttention

Prof. Rio Yokota · Institute of Science Tokyo

Why fusion wins, and how FlashAttention reshapes the memory bottleneck.

11:00 – 11:20

Tea break

11:20 – 13:00

Writing a Triton kernel

Invited engineer · GPU systems (USA)

From PyTorch op to a custom Triton kernel, with live benchmarking.

13:00 – 14:00

Lunch break own arrangement

14:00 – 17:30

Lab 2 — fuse & accelerate Hands-on

Lab leads & teaching assistants

Fuse kernels and apply FlashAttention to your model; profile the before/after on the cluster.

18:00 – 19:00

Optional evening clinic

09:00 – 10:30

Serving systems & dynamic batching

Prof. Purushottam Kulkarni · IIT Bombay

KV-cache management, continuous batching, Triton Inference Server, vLLM and Ray Serve architecture.

10:30 – 11:00

Autoscaling & cost-aware routing

Prof. Umesh Bellur · IIT Bombay

Scaling replicas to traffic and routing requests to minimise cost per query.

11:00 – 11:20

Tea break

11:20 – 13:00

Fireside chat: inference at industry scale Panel

International faculty & invited practitioners

What breaks in production, and how teams in Tokyo, the US and India are organised around it.

13:00 – 14:00

Lunch break own arrangement

14:00 – 17:30

Lab 3 — build a serving stack Hands-on

Lab leads & teaching assistants

Stand up a batched, KV-cached inference service with vLLM behind an autoscaling endpoint.

18:00 – 19:00

Optional evening clinic

09:00 – 10:30

Deploying LLMs at scale Intl. faculty

Prof. Song Han · MIT, USA

Paged attention, tensor & pipeline parallelism, and serving multi-billion-parameter models.

10:30 – 11:00

Speculative & parallel decoding

Prof. Rio Yokota · Institute of Science Tokyo

Speeding up generation with draft models and parallel decoding strategies.

11:00 – 11:20

Tea break

11:20 – 13:00

Production, MLOps & reliability

Invited engineer · MLOps (USA)

Observability, canary releases and drift detection for live inference services.

13:00 – 14:00

Lunch break own arrangement

14:00 – 17:30

Lab 4 — deploy & harden an LLM endpoint Hands-on

Lab leads & teaching assistants

Serve an LLM with paged attention, add monitoring, and load-test it. Capstone teams & brief announced.

18:00 – 19:00

Capstone kick-off & clinic

09:00 – 09:45

Capstone briefing & rules

All faculty & teaching assistants

The shared model, the metric, and the leaderboard explained.

09:45 – 13:00

Capstone — the inference leaderboard Hands-on

Teams · mentored by faculty & TAs

Teams optimise a shared model and compete on a live latency–throughput–cost leaderboard.

13:00 – 14:00

Lunch break own arrangement

14:00 – 15:30

Final optimisation sprint Hands-on

Last push on the leaderboard before submissions close.

15:30 – 16:30

Capstone presentations & judging Showcase

International judging panel

Top teams present their approach; judges score on the combined metric.

16:30 – 17:30

Valedictory, prizes & certificates

Convener & organising committee

Prizes, certificates and closing remarks. Group photograph.

Note: the schedule is indicative and may be adjusted. International-faculty sessions may run partly over live video where travel requires; exact speaker assignments for invited-industry sessions are confirmed closer to the event and announced by e-mail to registered participants.
