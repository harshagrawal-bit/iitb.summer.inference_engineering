About the ProgrammeMLIE 2026

Overview

Modern AI is bottlenecked not by training, but by the cost and latency of serving models at scale. MLIE 2026 is a three-day, lab-driven summer school that introduces the engineering discipline of inference — from first principles to deploying an optimised large-language-model endpoint of your own. Mornings are lecture-led; afternoons are supervised labs on the C-MInDS A100 GPU cluster; the school closes with a competitive optimisation capstone.

Who should attend

Open to students, scholars, research staff and faculty from all IITs, IISc, IIITs, NITs and recognised partner institutions, plus early-career engineers.

B.Tech / M.TechPhD scholarsResearch staffFacultyEarly-career engineers

Prerequisites: working knowledge of Python and a first course in machine learning. PyTorch and Linux familiarity helpful but not required.

Curriculum — six modules

01 · Foundations of Inference

Forward pass, latency vs. throughput, roofline analysis.

02 · Model Compression

INT8/INT4 quantization, pruning, distillation.

03 · GPU & Kernel Optimization

CUDA, memory coalescing, fusion, FlashAttention, Triton.

04 · Serving Systems

Dynamic batching, KV-cache, Triton, vLLM, Ray Serve.

05 · LLM Deployment at Scale

Paged attention, parallelism, speculative decoding.

06 · Production & MLOps

Observability, autoscaling, canary, drift detection.

Learning outcomes

  • Reason about performance and decide where optimisation pays off
  • Compress models and measure the size–speed–accuracy trade-off
  • Build a batched, KV-cached serving stack and deploy an LLM endpoint
  • Profile real GPU workloads and operate a service reliably
