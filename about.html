About the Summer School

A focused, lab-driven programme on the engineering that turns trained models into fast, reliable, affordable production services.

Overview

From research notebook to production endpoint

The hard part of modern machine learning is increasingly not training a model, but serving it — quickly, cheaply, and at scale, to real users.

The Summer School on Machine Learning Inference Engineering (MLIE 2026) is a three-day residential programme that introduces participants to this discipline end to end. It is jointly organised by the Centre for Machine Intelligence and Data Science (C-MInDS) and the Department of Computer Science & Engineering at IIT Bombay, under the Institute's Continuing Education Programme.

The school deliberately mixes theory and practice: each morning introduces the principles behind a topic, and each afternoon puts them to work in a supervised lab on the C-MInDS GPU cluster. By the end, every participant will have profiled, optimised and deployed a model of their own.

Learning outcomes

What you will be able to do

By the close of the school, participants will have the vocabulary and the hands-on muscle memory to take a model to production.

Reason about performance

Read a roofline plot, distinguish compute-bound from memory-bound workloads, and decide where optimisation effort will actually pay off.

Compress models confidently

Apply INT8/INT4 quantization, pruning and distillation, and measure the real trade-off between size, speed and accuracy.

Build a serving stack

Stand up a batched, KV-cached inference service with vLLM / Triton and expose it behind an autoscaling endpoint.

Profile on real GPUs

Use Nsight and PyTorch profiler to find bottlenecks and apply operator fusion and FlashAttention-style kernels.

Deploy an LLM

Serve a large language model with paged attention and parallelism, and reason about cost per million tokens.

Operate it reliably

Add monitoring, canary rollouts and drift detection so the service survives contact with real traffic.

How it's taught

Half lecture, half lab — and a competitive capstone

The format is intentionally intense and practical:

  • Morning lectures introduce the day's principles with worked examples and live demos.
  • Afternoon labs are supervised by faculty and teaching assistants, with individual GPU instances.
  • Evening clinics offer optional office-hours for debugging and deeper questions.
  • Day-3 capstone: teams optimise a shared model and compete on a public latency–throughput–cost leaderboard.

Teaching assistants maintain a 1:8 ratio in labs, so help is always close at hand.

Eligibility & prerequisites

Who can apply

Eligibility

Open to students, scholars, research staff and faculty from all IITs, IISc, IIITs, NITs and recognised partner institutions, as well as early-career professionals. A valid institute ID is required at check-in.

B.Tech / M.Tech PhD scholars Project staff Faculty Industry (early career)

Prerequisites

  • Comfortable programming in Python
  • A first course in machine learning (or equivalent self-study)
  • Helpful but optional: PyTorch, Linux command line, basic C/CUDA awareness
  • A laptop capable of SSH access to the GPU cluster

A short primer and setup guide is e-mailed to selected participants two weeks before the school.
